Tender question

Do I need a Carbon Reduction Plan to bid? It depends on the buyer and the contract size.

By Rob Atkins, Founder & Principal Consultant

On central government contracts worth more than £5 million a year including VAT, usually yes. From 1 April 2027, NHS rules reach further, down to procurements below £5 million a year that are above the published procurement thresholds. Elsewhere it's down to the buyer. Here's the part worth knowing: under the central government guidance, the plan is pass or fail and your emissions figures aren't what's being assessed. A foundry with a big footprint can pass. A clean assembly shop with an out-of-date plan can fail. So what you need is a plan that's complete, current and signed off, and that takes data most manufacturers haven't pulled together.

Who is likely to ask for one

Central government

PPN 006 applies to departments, their agencies and non-departmental public bodies on contracts above £5 million a year including VAT, unless it isn't relevant or proportionate. The value is averaged over the contract's life, so a four-year contract worth £21 million is in scope.

The NHS, from 1 April 2027

At £5 million a year or more, and on new frameworks and dynamic markets by default, the 2027 NHS plan applies and covers all relevant Scope 3 emissions. Below £5 million but above the procurement thresholds, a plan with the shorter Scope 3 list applies. Procurements started earlier stay under the old rules.

Wales, councils and other buyers

Wales has its own policy note, WPPN 006. Councils and other public bodies can set their own requirements, so read the conditions of participation on each tender.

Primes and larger customers

If a bigger customer needs to report on its supply chain, or is bidding for public work itself, it may ask you for a plan as a condition of doing business, even when your own contract is well under £5 million.

What gets a plan failed

1. Nothing on net zero

The plan has to commit to net zero by 2050 at the latest for your UK operations. Leave that out and it fails, however good the rest of it is. Carbon Reduction Plans and ESG

2. Missing Scope 3 categories

You need all of Scope 1 and 2, plus five Scope 3 categories: upstream transport, waste from your operations, business travel, employee commuting and downstream transport. For a manufacturer, that means inbound freight and deliveries to customers. Leaving out a required Scope without explaining why is a fail.

3. Out-of-date figures

A reporting period more than 18 months old fails unless you can explain why. The plan should be reviewed and updated every year, within six months of your financial year-end, so one you wrote two years ago for a tender may no longer count.

4. No sign-off, or nowhere to find it

The board has to approve it and a director has to sign it. It also has to set out your environmental management measures, and be published on your UK website with a link in a prominent place on your homepage.

Why a last-minute template doesn't work

You can download the government's template in a minute. Filling it in is another matter, because it needs a full year of energy bills, fuel, waste, freight, business travel and commuting figures, and those usually sit with different people, if anyone's kept them at all.

Start when the tender lands and you could find yourself working out a year's emissions against a closing date. And you'll need to do it again next year.

Common questions about Carbon Reduction Plans

Is a Carbon Reduction Plan a legal requirement?

No general law says every manufacturer must have one. It's a condition buyers set for taking part in a tender. Larger companies may have separate carbon reporting duties under Streamlined Energy and Carbon Reporting, so check that with your accountant.

Are our emissions figures scored?

Not under the central government guidance. The compliance questions are pass or fail, and your baseline and current emissions are for information only. Other buyers can treat it differently, and carbon commitments can also come up in social value or contract terms, so read each tender's criteria.

Can we use our parent company's plan?

Under PPN 006, only in limited circumstances: the bidder must be wholly owned by the parent, adopt its commitment and publish the plan on its own website. PPN 006 treats this as a temporary measure until the bidder has a plan of its own.

What if we've been trading for less than a year?

The guidance says suppliers trading for under 12 months shouldn't be disadvantaged. You should confirm your commitment to net zero by 2050 and publish a full plan as soon as you can.

Do we need one if we're bidding as a consortium?

Yes. The central government guidance says each consortium member should complete its own plan, so check your partners have theirs in good time.

Will a central government plan meet the NHS rules?

Not necessarily. From 1 April 2027, the NHS plan for procurements of £5 million a year or more needs all relevant Scope 3 emissions, not just the five categories, and has its own rules on net zero. Check the NHS England guidance that applies to your tender.
