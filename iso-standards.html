What it looks like inside a manufacturer

  • Processes are controlled, and responsibilities are defined.
  • Procedures are documented, and people actually follow them.
  • Training and competence records are current.
  • Calibration and maintenance logs are up to date.
  • Suppliers are assured, not assumed.
  • Non-conformances are managed rather than argued about.
  • Audit trails exist and stand up to scrutiny.
  • Environmental controls and safety risk assessments are in place.
  • Incidents get reported and reviewed.
  • Management oversight is documented, not implied.

What it costs you to leave alone

Without ISO, a buyer assumes inconsistent processes, uncontrolled risks, weak governance, unreliable delivery, poor evidence and high operational volatility.

None of that may be true of you. It does not matter. In a tender, the assumption is what gets scored.

The point most people miss

Buyers, especially OEMs and Tier-1s, use ISO as a risk filter. If you have it, you are low risk. If you do not, you are high risk, regardless of how good you actually are.

People think ISO is paperwork. It is not. ISO is predictability, and predictability is what buyers want and pay for.
