ISO is the discipline that proves you are controlled, consistent, safe and audit-ready. In the Cure Alliance model it is the backbone, because almost every other service leans on the evidence it produces.

What it costs you to leave alone

Without ISO, a buyer assumes inconsistent processes, uncontrolled risks, weak governance, unreliable delivery, poor evidence and high operational volatility.

None of that may be true of you. It does not matter. In a tender, the assumption is what gets scored.

The point most people miss

Buyers, especially OEMs and Tier-1s, use ISO as a risk filter. If you have it, you are low risk. If you do not, you are high risk, regardless of how good you actually are.

People think ISO is paperwork. It is not. ISO is predictability, and predictability is what buyers want and pay for.

What it looks like inside a manufacturer

  • Processes are controlled, and responsibilities are defined.
  • Procedures are documented, and people actually follow them.
  • Training and competence records are current.
  • Calibration and maintenance logs are up to date.
  • Suppliers are assured, not assumed.
  • Non-conformances are managed rather than argued about.
  • Audit trails exist and stand up to scrutiny.
  • Environmental controls and safety risk assessments are in place.
  • Incidents get reported and reviewed.
  • Management oversight is documented, not implied.

Common questions about ISO standards

What is ISO 9001:2026, and do I need to upgrade?

ISO 9001:2026 is the new version of the quality standard, published on 16 September 2026. Certified organisations have until 30 September 2029 to transition, after which ISO 9001:2015 certificates expire or are withdrawn. The main changes are quality culture and ethical behaviour, separate requirements for risks and opportunities, and stronger change management. Plan the move at your next recertification rather than leaving it to the deadline.

How long does ISO certification take?

For most small and mid-sized manufacturers, ISO 9001 takes three to six months, ISO 14001 and ISO 45001 four to six months, and ISO 27001 six to twelve months. Certificates then run on a three-year cycle with annual surveillance audits. Start at least six months before the tender you are aiming for.

Which ISO standards do manufacturing buyers ask for?

ISO 9001 is the one asked for most, because buyers use it as a risk filter for quality. ISO 14001 covers environmental management, ISO 45001 health and safety, and ISO 27001 information security where sensitive data is shared. Some OEMs and Tier-1s also expect sector standards, such as IATF 16949 in automotive or AS9100 in aerospace.
