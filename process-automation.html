What it looks like inside a manufacturer

  • Records and evidence can be retrieved quickly when a tender or audit asks for them.
  • SharePoint and OneDrive are structured, not a folder free-for-all.
  • Repetitive administrative tasks run through an automated workflow.
  • Version control means everyone is working from the current document.
  • Key processes are documented well enough that they don't rely on one person.
  • Approval workflows are traceable, with a clear record of who signed off what.
  • Process documentation is current and matches how work actually happens.

What it costs you to leave alone

Without process automation, a buyer rarely notices directly — but the business feels it evidence takes too long to produce when a tender or audit asks for it, processes depend on one person's memory or method, version control errors create inconsistent documentation, administrative errors creep into repeated manual tasks and audits take longer and surface more inconsistencies than they should.

None of this typically loses a specific tender on its own — but it quietly undermines every other workstream's evidence.

The point most people miss

Most manufacturers can describe how work gets done. Few can produce the evidence of it happening that way, on the day an auditor or evaluator actually asks.

A process that lives in one person's head is not a process. It is a single point of failure with a job title.
