Insurance Broking & Key Person Cover

Proving the financial hit lands on your insurer, not theirs.

Department: Risk Surgery and Preventative Medicine

When something goes wrong on a contract, the buyer wants to know the financial hit lands on your insurer, not on them. Cover levels and formats get checked early, and mechanically.

What it costs you to leave alone

Without correctly structured insurance, a buyer assumes underinsured exposure, a business one bad event away from collapse, no continuity plan if a key person leaves or falls ill, mismatched liability between contract and cover and administrative carelessness that suggests wider carelessness.

None of that may be true of you. It does not matter. In a tender, the assumption is what gets scored.

The point most people miss

Most manufacturers assume their broker sorted this years ago and never revisit it. Cover quietly falls out of date while the business, and the contracts it bids for, grow past it.

Insurance is not a policy document in a drawer. It is proof that the business is professionally run.

What it covers

Core Business Cover

Public liability, employers' liability, professional indemnity and product liability at buyer-specified minimums.

Cyber & Continuity

Cyber liability aligned to your certification, plus business interruption and machinery breakdown cover.

Key Person Protection

Cover for the founder or critical-skill dependency that would stop delivery if they were gone.

Evidence & Renewal

Cover checked against every tender, certificates in the right format, renewals tracked against the pipeline.

What it looks like inside a manufacturer

  • Cover levels are checked against each tender before submission, not assumed.
  • Certificates are in the exact format the procurement portal requires.
  • Key person cover exists for anyone whose absence would stop delivery.
  • Cyber and PI cover are aligned with actual contractual liability.
  • Machinery breakdown cover is in place for the equipment that would stop you.
  • Renewal dates are tracked against the bid pipeline, not just the calendar.
  • Insurance schedules are reviewed annually against business growth.
  • Claims history is documented and explainable.
