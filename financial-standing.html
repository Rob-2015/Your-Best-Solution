Chartered Accountant

Proving you'll still be trading when the contract ends.

Department: Vital Signs and Cardiac Monitoring

Financial standing is the first thing a buyer checks, and it is often automated. A threshold gets set — typically twice the annual contract value — and bids below it never reach a human reader.

What it costs you to leave alone

Without current, well-presented financials, a buyer assumes insufficient turnover to safely deliver the contract, cash flow that can't absorb mobilisation costs, outdated or unreliable financial reporting, undisclosed tax or compliance risk and a business growing faster than its financial controls.

None of that may be true of you. It does not matter. In a tender, the assumption is what gets scored.

The point most people miss

Most manufacturers think their accountant's job ends at the tax return. It does not. The moment you bid for a contract worth more than half your turnover, your books stop being paperwork and start being evidence.

Nobody negotiates with a financial threshold. You either clear it or you are filtered out before anyone reads a word you wrote.

Financial Standing

Turnover thresholds, filed accounts and management accounts that clear the buyer's minimum bar.

What it looks like inside a manufacturer

  • Filed accounts are current and match what's declared in the bid.
  • Management accounts exist and are current if statutory accounts lag.
  • Turnover comfortably clears the threshold buyers set against contract value.
  • Tax compliance is confirmed and evidenced, not just assumed.
  • Financial ratios are monitored, not discovered for the first time in a tender.
  • Cash flow is modelled against contract mobilisation costs.
  • Credit score is checked and understood before it surprises anyone.
  • Financial documentation is ready to submit without a scramble.
