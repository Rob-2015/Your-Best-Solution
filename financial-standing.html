Department: Vital Signs and Cardiac Monitoring

Financial standing is the first thing a buyer checks, and it is often automated. A threshold gets set — typically twice the annual contract value — and bids below it never reach a human reader.

What it costs you to leave alone

Without current, well-presented financials, a buyer assumes insufficient turnover to safely deliver the contract, cash flow that can't absorb mobilisation costs, outdated or unreliable financial reporting, undisclosed tax or compliance risk and a business growing faster than its financial controls.

None of that may be true of you. It does not matter. In a tender, the assumption is what gets scored.

The point most people miss

Most manufacturers think their accountant's job ends at the tax return. It does not. The moment you bid for a contract worth more than half your turnover, your books stop being paperwork and start being evidence.

Nobody negotiates with a financial threshold. You either clear it or you are filtered out before anyone reads a word you wrote.

Common questions about financial standing

How much turnover do I need to bid for a contract?

Buyers commonly set a minimum turnover of around twice the annual contract value, and many check it automatically. If a contract is worth more than half your turnover, expect your accounts to be scrutinised closely. Check the selection questionnaire for the exact figure on every tender, because it varies.

What financial evidence do buyers ask for?

Usually your last two or three years of filed accounts, plus management accounts if your statutory accounts are out of date. Many buyers also run a credit check through a credit reference agency, and some ask for a bank reference. Make sure what you declare in the bid matches exactly what Companies House shows.

Can a young or fast-growing manufacturer pass financial standing?

Often, yes, if you prepare for it. Current management accounts, a parent company guarantee or a performance bond can all help, and a short, honest explanation of a one-off dip reads far better than silence. The mistake is letting an automated check make the decision before anyone sees the context.
