Health & Safety

Proving your site won't hurt anyone, on paper and on the floor.

Department: Accident and Emergency

Health and safety is checked on almost every public-sector tender, and it is checked hard. A missing or lapsed accreditation is one of the fastest ways an otherwise strong bid gets screened out.

What it costs you to leave alone

Without current H&S accreditation, a buyer assumes an uncontrolled or poorly supervised site, higher likelihood of incidents and delivery disruption, weak duty-of-care toward your own people, accreditation that has lapsed without anyone noticing and a business that reacts to incidents rather than preventing them.

None of that may be true of you. It does not matter. In a tender, the assumption is what gets scored.

The point most people miss

Most manufacturers think of H&S as paperwork that sits in a folder. Auditors do not read the folder first. They walk the floor, then check whether the paperwork matches what they just saw.

Buyers do not want a policy document. They want proof that if something goes wrong on your shop floor, you already know what happens next.

What it covers

Policy & Risk Assessment

Written H&S policy, risk assessments and method statements specific to your actual operations.

Accreditation

SafeContractor, Achilles or an equivalent SSIP scheme, current and matched to the accrediting body's register.

Incident Management

RIDDOR reporting, accident tracking and corrective actions tracked to closure.

Site Discipline

PPE, inductions, machinery inspection records and a named person with real authority to act.

What it looks like inside a manufacturer

  • Risk assessments are specific to the actual operations, not generic templates.
  • Accreditation certificates are current and match the accrediting body's register.
  • Accident and near-miss logs are maintained and reviewed, not filed and forgotten.
  • Machinery has current maintenance and safety inspection records.
  • Staff can describe the evacuation plan without prompting.
  • Inductions happen before anyone new sets foot on the shop floor.
  • Corrective actions from incidents are tracked to closure.
  • A named person owns H&S with real authority to act.

Common questions about health and safety

Which health and safety accreditation do I need for tenders?

Most buyers accept a scheme that belongs to Safety Schemes in Procurement (SSIP), such as CHAS, SafeContractor or Achilles. Some ask for ISO 45001 instead, particularly on larger or higher-risk contracts. Check which one the buyer names before you spend money, because the right single certificate often covers several buyers.

What does SSIP deem to satisfy mean?

It means SSIP member schemes recognise each other's core health and safety assessment. If you hold one SSIP certificate and a buyer asks for a different SSIP scheme, you can usually get recognised without paying for a full second assessment. It saves time and money, but only if your certificate is current and shows on the scheme's register.

What health and safety evidence do evaluators actually check?

Proof that your paperwork matches your shop floor. That means risk assessments specific to your operations, an accident and near-miss log, RIDDOR reports where required, machinery inspection records, training records and a current certificate that matches the accrediting body's register. Generic templates are spotted quickly.
